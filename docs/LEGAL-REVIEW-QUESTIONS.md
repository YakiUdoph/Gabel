# Questions for qualified legal review

No universal compliance conclusion is made. Counsel familiar with the operator, deployment location, and intended users should determine:

1. The correct legal identity/address of the operator and whether additional company/contact disclosures are required.
2. Which privacy laws apply based on operator establishment, user locations, targeting, scale, and data roles; the lawful bases/notice wording and processor/controller characterization for Buffer and hosting vendors.
3. Required procedures, identity verification, response periods, exceptions, appeal rights, and backup/log deletion for access/deletion/other data-subject requests.
4. Whether strictly necessary session/CSRF cookies need a banner, consent, or only disclosure in each launch jurisdiction.
5. Required contract terms: governing law, venue, consumer rights, disclaimers, liability limits, indemnities, service availability, and termination.
6. Whether a data-processing agreement, subprocessor list, international-transfer mechanism, security addendum, or breach-notification process is needed.
7. Minimum-age/age-assurance rules for the intended audience and whether the service should impose an age threshold or parental-consent mechanism. Gabel should not knowingly collect children's data before this is resolved.
8. Content/IP, publicity, employment, sector-specific, and social-platform obligations arising from users editing scheduled media.
9. Accessibility obligations beyond WCAG 2.2 AA technical guidance in the markets where the service is offered.
10. Recordkeeping/retention obligations that could require changes to the proposed 10-minute/24-hour/30-day schedule.
11. Whether incident response, vulnerability reporting, sanctions/export, acceptable-use, or law-enforcement request policies are required.
12. Re-review triggers if Gabel adds payments, subscriptions, analytics, advertising, marketing email, new processors, new data categories, automated monitoring/repair, or users in new jurisdictions.
